The Israeli Wine Region that runs through the heart of the country

Stand at 800 meters on the Central Mountain range and you get cool mountain air, stone terraces, even snow some winters. Drive a short way east and the same ridge drops into the Judean Desert, down to the Dead Sea, the lowest point on Earth.

That ridge runs the length of the country, from Mt. Gilboa through the Shomron hills to the Hebron mountains, and it's the watershed divide that decides which way the rain falls. Its wine story starts just outside Jerusalem: Domaine du Castel planted there in 1992, Tzora Vineyards followed in 1993, and together they made the Judean Hills one of the most talked-about addresses in Israeli wine.

I wanted to show you how altitude, stony ground and the choices of individual winemakers shape the wine here, and why these wines are definitely worth experiencing.
