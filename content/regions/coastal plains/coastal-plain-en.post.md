## TITLE
Where It All Started: Wine on the Coastal Plain


## BODY

This is where the modern Israeli wine industry was born - and some of its oldest vines are still in the ground to prove it.

**At a Glance**
Where: the coastal strip along the Mediterranean, from the Zichron Ya'acov-Hanadiv Valley down through the Judean Coast, mostly 0-150m above sea level.
Climate: warm and humid, tempered by cool sea breezes off the Mediterranean.
Soil: coastal sands and loams, shaped by the sea's proximity as much as by the ground itself.


**The Story**

In the 1880s, Baron Edmond de Rothschild set out to restart Israel's wine industry almost from scratch, and he chose the coast to do it. His investment centered on Zichron Ya'acov, and by 1892 Carmel had built its cellars there - cellars that are still in use today, making this the country's oldest continuously producing modern wine region.

That founding choice still shapes the coast. Because the industry took root here first, and because change comes slowly once vines are in the ground, many of Israel's oldest vineyards are still planted along this strip. A lot of them are Carignan, a grape that arrived early and never left, giving the Coastal Plain some of the oldest vines in the country.

The coast's own ancient history predates all of this by well over a thousand years. In antiquity, this was wine country too, and on a serious scale - the largest wine presses found anywhere in the region turned up here, including a genuinely huge one at Yavneh, likely built so close to the ports because the wine was headed for export. One ancient grape variety, since named Yael, was identified near Ashkelon and is grown in commercial wine again today - a quiet thread connecting the region's ancient trade in wine to its modern one.


**Why the Coast Grows Differently**

Low altitude and humidity aren't usually a winemaker's first choice, and today the Coastal Plain isn't considered Israel's most ideal wine terrain for that reason. But the sea doesn't just add humidity - it also tempers the heat, with cool Mediterranean breezes moving steadily across the vineyards and taking the edge off what would otherwise be a much hotter growing season.

Once modern viticulture had the data to show which grapes actually suit that combination of warmth and humidity, the coast shifted its planting accordingly. Heat-tolerant varieties took over from grapes better suited to cooler ground: Carignan (already there from the region's early decades), along with Syrah, Marselan, and others bred or chosen specifically to handle the coast's conditions without losing quality. It's a region that adapted to what its own climate actually wants, rather than fighting it.


**What to Expect in the Glass**

Old-vine Carignan is the Coastal Plain's real signature - vines old enough to give real depth and concentration, from a grape that's often treated as a workhorse elsewhere but shows real character here. Alongside it, the newer heat-adapted plantings (Syrah, Marselan) bring riper, warmer-climate character: bolder fruit, fuller body, less of the high-altitude freshness you'd find in a Galilee or Golan wine. It's a style shaped honestly by its own conditions rather than working against them.


**Wineries to Visit**

- Tishbi - well known for its restaurant and chocolates alongside the wine.
- Binyamina - one of the coast's established names.
- Dadah
- Haroe - a tiny, personal boutique experience.
