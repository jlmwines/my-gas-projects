The Israeli Wine Region That Gets 1,000mm of Rain a Year

Israel is mostly a hot, dry country. Up in the Galilee's highest vineyards (some reaching 1,200m), it isn't.

From the mid-1990s on, growers planting the Upper Galilee's higher slopes found something rare here: real altitude, up to 1,000mm of rain a year, and cool nights that hold onto freshness most of the country never sees. The soil shifts from terra rossa to volcanic basalt to limestone within a few hundred metres, so no single style dominates: you'll find structured reds built for aging right alongside brighter, more aromatic whites, often from the same producer.

I wanted you to see how altitude and rain quietly became Israel's best argument for cool-climate wine, and which producers up there are worth exploring.
