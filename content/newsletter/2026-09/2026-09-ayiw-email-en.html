Making Wine - Harvest Time

In the vineyard:

Red wine harvest is in full swing. We’re deeper into the season and starting to see the end. We’re constantly checking each plot to see when the ideal harvest time will be. We do this by sampling each plot to check sugar levels, acidity and phenolic ripeness - how full of flavor and ready it tastes.

In the cellar:

Many of the grapes harvested in August are still fermenting, and some are done with their fermentation. This also needs constant monitoring to decide when each wine should be pressed and which wines go into which tanks.

While alcoholic fermentation requires oxygen for it to happen, once the fermentation is done, contact with oxygen needs to become limited. We also need to separate the wine from the leftover grape remnants and slowly start clarifying the wines. Most of this process is done using gravity which pulls down the remnants and racking the wine above that.

While all this action is happening, last year's reds are still aging quietly in their barrels. We taste them for development and top up the barrels to prevent oxidation.
